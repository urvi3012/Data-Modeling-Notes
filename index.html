Data Modeling & The Ecosystem

Data modeling is the architectural bridge between raw information and business intelligence. It defines how data points relate to one another and how they are physically stored. In Data Engineering, modeling is split into two worlds: OLTP for capturing data and OLAP for analyzing it.

1. OLTP vs. OLAP

OLTP (Online Transactional Processing)

OLTP systems are the Source of data. They are designed for high-concurrency, fast-paced "writes." When you buy a coffee or update your profile, an OLTP database (like Postgres) handles that single row change instantly.

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)

OLAP systems are the Destination. They are designed for massive "reads." Instead of looking at one customer, OLAP scans millions of customers to find trends. It is the core of modern Data Warehousing.

3. Fact & Dimension Table Types

The Gold layer organizes data into Facts (Numbers) and Dimensions (Attributes).

Accumulating Snapshot Fact

Unlike a Transactional Fact (which records one event), an Accumulating Fact tracks a lifecycle. Each row represents a single item, but multiple columns track its progress through different dates.

4. Surrogate Keys

A Surrogate Key is a system-generated unique identifier (usually an integer like 1, 2, 3) used as the Primary Key in a dimension table.

Why use them instead of Natural Keys (like Email)?

  • Stability: If a user changes their email, the Surrogate Key stays the same, keeping historical joins intact.
  • Performance: Joining on a small 4-byte integer is much faster than joining on a 50-character string.
  • SCD Management: Required for SCD Type 2 tracking, where one natural ID might have multiple rows.

5. Star vs. Snowflake Schema

Star Schema: The simplest model where all dimensions connect directly to a central fact. Fastest performance.

Snowflake Schema: A normalized model where dimensions connect to other sub-dimensions. Saves space but slows queries due to more joins.
