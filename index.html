Data Modeling

Data modeling is the architectural blueprint of how data is stored, connected, and accessed. In Data Engineering, modeling bridges the gap between raw application logs and business intelligence.

Modeling ensures Data Integrity (it is correct), Performance (it is fast to query), and Cost Efficiency (it doesn't waste storage).

1. OLTP vs. OLAP

To build a data platform, you must understand the difference between the source systems (OLTP) and the analytical systems you build (OLAP).

OLTP (Online Transactional Processing)

These are "Operational" systems. They handle the daily transactions of a business (e.g., an ATM, a checkout counter, or a social media 'like'). They are optimized for High-Write speed and ACID compliance.

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)

These are "Analytical" systems. They are built for business intelligence. Instead of looking at a single order, OLAP scans millions of rows to find trends (e.g., "Monthly Revenue by Region"). They are optimized for High-Read speed.

3. Fact & Dimension Table Types

In the Gold layer, we separate data into Facts (Numbers) and Dimensions (Context). This structure is designed to answer "How much?" (Facts) and "By whom/when/where?" (Dimensions).

Fact Tables

Facts are the "events" or "measures."

  • Transactional: One row per event (e.g., A sale).
  • Periodic Snapshot: Aggregated views over time (e.g., Monthly balance).
  • Accumulating Snapshot: Tracks a lifecycle (e.g., Order -> Shipped -> Delivered).

4. Surrogate Keys

A Surrogate Key is a system-generated unique identifier (like 1, 2, 3...) used as the Primary Key in a dimension table.

Why use them? 1. Performance (Joining on integers is faster than strings like Email). 2. Stability (If a customer changes their email, your foreign keys don't break). 3. Historical tracking (Essential for SCD Type 2).

5. Star vs. Snowflake Schema

Star Schema: Dimensions connect directly to the central Fact table. It is fast and simple.

Snowflake Schema: Dimensions are normalized into sub-dimensions. It saves space but requires more complex joins.
