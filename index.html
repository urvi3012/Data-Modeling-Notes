Data Modeling

Data modeling is the process of creating a visual and structural blueprint that defines how data is stored, organized, and related within a database. For a Data Engineer, it isn't just about storage; it's about optimizing for query speed, cost, and reliability.

The 3 Phases of Modeling

  1. Conceptual Model: The "Big Picture." Defines business entities (e.g., Customer, Product) and how they connect without any technical jargon.
  2. Logical Model: The "Plan." Adds attributes (Email, Price) and relationships (Primary/Foreign Keys) but is still independent of the database type.
  3. Physical Model: The "Implementation." Specific to the database (Snowflake, BigQuery). Includes data types, indexing strategies, and partitioning.

1. OLTP vs. OLAP

Data engineers act as the bridge between Operational systems (where data is born) and Analytical systems (where data is used).

Feature OLTP (Transactional) OLAP (Analytical)
PurposeSupports daily business operations (e.g., Banking, Checkout).Supports decision-making and historic reporting.
Data FormatNormalized (3NF): Prevents redundancy by splitting data into many tables.Denormalized: Blends tables together to reduce "joins" for fast reads.
Write/ReadHeavy on small, fast writes (Inserts/Updates).Heavy on massive, complex read scans.
Example ProductsPostgres, MySQL, Oracle, MongoDB.Snowflake, BigQuery, AWS Redshift, Databricks.
RequirementsACID compliance, High availability.Query performance, Massive horizontal scaling.

3. Gold Layer: Dimensional Modeling

The Gold layer is where One Big Table (OBT) from Silver is broken down into Facts and Dimensions. This follows the Kimball Methodology.

Fact Tables (The Numbers)

Fact tables store quantitative metrics (Measures) and Foreign Keys. They are usually long and thin.

  • Transactional Fact: Records a single event (One row per sale).
  • Periodic Fact: Summarizes data over time (Weekly total revenue).
  • Accumulating Fact: Tracks a lifecycle (Order -> Shipping -> Delivery).

Dimension Tables (The Context)

Dimension tables store descriptive attributes (Who, What, Where). They are wide and descriptive.

  • Conformed: Used across multiple Facts (e.g., A shared 'Date' dimension).
  • Role-Playing: One dimension used twice in a Fact (e.g., 'Date' used for Order_Date and Ship_Date).
